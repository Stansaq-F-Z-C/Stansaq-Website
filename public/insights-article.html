Specifying cast PU elastomers for high-abrasion rubber components

What changes when a rubber compounder substitutes a cast polyurethane system, and where the specification work actually sits.

FIG — Cast PU pour, elastomer mould

A cast polyurethane elastomer is not a drop-in replacement for a conventional rubber compound, even where hardness and colour are matched on a data sheet. The processing route differs — casting rather than moulding a pre-formed compound — and that difference shows up first in tooling and cycle time, before it shows up in part performance.

For components subject to sustained abrasion, the relevant comparison is not hardness alone but the combination of tear strength, resilience and abrasion resistance at the expected operating temperature. Cast PU systems typically hold an advantage in abrasion and tear resistance at moderate hardness, which is why they are frequently specified for components such as scraper blades, rollers and wear liners.

Where the specification work sits

In practice, the qualification step is where most of the engineering time goes: matching the correct polyol and isocyanate system to the part's service temperature, load cycle and chemical exposure, then validating that combination against a physical sample rather than a catalogue value.

This is also where a representation-model supplier differs from a pure trading operation. Because the technical team qualifying the material has direct access to the manufacturer's formulation data, a compounder can get a specific recommendation against their operating conditions, rather than a generic product range.

Practical checks before committing to a substitution

  • Confirm service temperature range against the candidate PU system's stated limits, not just room-temperature data
  • Request abrasion and tear data at the actual part hardness, not a representative grade
  • Trial-cast a full-scale sample rather than relying on a small test plaque
  • Check chemical exposure — some PU systems are unsuitable for sustained hydrocarbon or strong acid/alkali contact
